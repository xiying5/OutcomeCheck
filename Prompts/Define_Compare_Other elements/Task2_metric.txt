Prompt 2: Metric
# Guidelines for Identifying and Defining "Metric"
-In clinical trials, outcomes are defined using multiple elements. "Metric" is one of those elements.

## 1. Definition of "Metric": The unit of measuring scores at the individual participant level. Possible answers: “End value”, “Duration”, “Variability or average per person”, “Change from baseline”, “Events per person”, “Rate of change (repeated measures)”, “Time-to-event”, “Sum per person”, “Maximum per person”, or “Minimum per person”.
-Sometimes "Not reported" or “Not valid”.

IMPORTANT: Focus on participant-level measurement.

## 2. Definitions of possible answers

### A.”End value”: A single count or score per person, not computed based on other values
-Only one value per participant is used in the analysis at a time.
-Does not involve calculations across different time points (e.g., change, average, sum, maximum, minimum, or slope).
-For continuous and categorical variables, often a participant’s score on a test or questionnaire at the end of the study.
-For binary variables, often the risk of an event or the proportion who score above or below a threshold.
-A single value or timepoint alone is not sufficient. It needs to not involve calculations across two or more time points (i.e., not a change, average, sum, maximum, minimum, or slope in a participant’s score).
-Time is not part of the outcome definition.
-Answer “End value” if the outcome measures the “cumulative incidence” of an event and there is no explicit indication that the outcome will be assessed using time-to-event analysis.
-Answer "End value” if the outcome measures the proportion, percentage, or number of participants with a change in the status of a directly observable binary variable (e.g., occurrence of an incident event, completion or initiation of an action) between baseline and a second time point and all participants must have had the same baseline value. This alone is not sufficient, the outcome measure must clearly not be “Time-to-event”.
-Answer “Time-to-event” rather than “End value” if the “Outcome measure” or “Outcome description” indicates that “Time-to-event” analyses will be conducted in addition to comparing the proportion of people with the event.


#### Examples
-Influenza vaccine antibodies post-vaccination
-Recruitment rate
-Cumulative incidence of mortality
-Proportion of participants with new onset type 2 diabetes
-Number of participants with adverse events

### B. "Duration": A continuous variable representing the amount of time per person
-The outcome is defined by the duration or length of time, or the proportion of time, during which an event or activity occurs or does not occur for each participant, and the event status can be observed for all participants during the study period.
-The answer is not “Duration” if the outcome assesses the number of occurrences of an event within a period of time.

#### Examples
-Length of hospital stay
-Duration of medication use
-Proportion of days with pain
-Number of drinking days per week

### C. "Variability or average per person": The variability or average of a continuous measure per person
-The outcome in each participant is defined by calculating the variability or average of the value measured in the same individual during multiple intervals.
-Not used for outcomes defined by the amount or proportion of time (e.g., proportion of days)
-Must be the variability or average at the individual level
-Count is not continuous

#### Examples of variability or average per person
-Variability in stride length
-Mean Amplitude of Glycemic Excursions (MAGE)

#### Examples of counts that are not variability or average per person
-Drinks per week
-Daily step count

### D. "Change from baseline": Difference between two timepoints per person
-Difference between an individual participant’s score at baseline and a second timepoint is used for analysis.
-Might be a change in continuous or ordinal scale, or change in a categorical value. 
-Binary outcomes might be defined by change in individual participant scores when participants do not have the same baseline value.
-Calculating “Change from baseline” can be indicated by words like “change”, “reduction”, “decrease”, “improvement”, “maintain”, “increase”, “enlargement”, “loss”, “gain”, “worsening”, or “growth”.
-Answer “Change from baseline” for outcomes defined by the proportion of participants with a specified level of change in a continuous or ordinal outcome (e.g., proportion of participants with 50% improvement).
-The answer might be “Change from baseline” if the “Outcome measure” or “Outcome description” describes change even if the “Timeframe” mentions only one time (i.e., 0 or “baseline” might be implied).
-The presence of 2 or more timepoints does not necessarily suggest that the “Metric” is “Change from baseline”.
-”Change from baseline” refers to change within a participant, not the  “change” between the treatment and control group.

#### Examples:
-Change From Baseline in Lumbar Spine Bone Mineral Density (BMD)
-Proportion of patients who achieve at least 50% reduction in pain
-% change in forced vital capacity (FVC)
-​​Average participant change in body mass index
-Absolute Lymphocyte Count change score

### E. "Rate of change (repeated measures)”: The speed of change over time per person (3 or more timepoints)
-Requires explicit information about using “repeated measures” in a single analysis to calculate the “rate of change” or “slope” (e.g., analyzed by mixed effect models). 
-The “Timeframe” must include at least 2 timepoints in addition to time 0 or “baseline” (which might be implied), or the outcome must be assessed “Up to” or “Until” a specified time.
-Use of 3 or more timepoints is necessary but not sufficient. 

#### Examples:
-Mean change in A1c per month (slope)
-Annualized Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) Slope Over Time
-Rate of change in Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39 score
-Repeated measures analysis of change in depression scores

#### Exceptions:
-Use "End value” if separate analyses are done at each timepoint.
-Use "Change from baseline" if separate analyses on the change from baseline are done at each of the different timepoints.
-Never use with only 1 or 2 timepoints.
-Use "Change from baseline" if the outcome is defined by the slope between exactly 2  time points.


### F. “Events per person”: The sum of discrete events per person
#### F.1 First, determine whether the event would be recorded/analyzed once or multiple times for a person in a clinical trial. The outcomes you assess were all predefined in clinical trials and assessed within the specified timeframe. 

##### Examples of events that would be recorded/analyzed only once per person in a RCT:
-Occurrence of an irreversible outcome (e.g., birth, death, permanent cessation of a status)
-First occurrence or onset of a chronic health condition (e.g., recurrence of breast cancer, incident upper respiratory infection, new onset diabetes)
-Permanent change in study status (e.g., treatment discontinuation, study withdrawal)
-An end value above or below a threshold on a continuous scale (e.g., 50% reduction in pain)

#####  Examples of events that could be recorded multiple times per person in a RCT:
-Falls
-Steps
-Asthma attacks
-Migraines
-Doctor visits
-Adverse events
-Smoking cigarettes

-Do not answer “Events per person” for outcomes that can happen to a person only once in a clinical trial.

#### F.2 Then consider whether the outcome matches the following definition of “Events per person”:
-The number or average number of times that an event or discrete activities occurs per trial participant.
-Used for counts of events or discrete activities that can occur multiple times in a participant (e.g., falling).
-The total number of binary events per participant during a trial, or the average number of discrete occurrences per participant over a period of time.
-Implied by statistical terms or methods for count data (e.g., Poisson, Negative binomial).
-Not used for composite outcomes that can occur only once for a single participant.
-Not used for cumulative incidence (risk) in a group of participants.
-Not used for the number of people with an event.
-Not used for sum of time units (e.g., number of minutes, proportion of days).

#### Examples
-Number of sessions completed per participant
-Proportion of intervention sessions attended per participant
-Average daily step count
-Number of falls per person during the study period
-Number of times the anesthesiologist adjusts ventilator settings

### G. ”Time-to-event”: Represents both length of time and event status (i.e., the length of time until an event of interest or censoring occurs in each person)

#### G.1 First consider whether the event can occur more than once for a person in a clinical trial following the instructions above (see F.1).

####  G.2 Then consider whether the outcome matches the following definition of “Time-to-Event”
-Used only for events that can occur “Once” in a participant.
-Not all participants necessarily experience the event during the study period.
-The outcome is defined as the time until an event occurs (e.g., “time from baseline to treatment discontinuation”), or statistical terms or tests used for time-to-event analysis  indicate the metric is "Time-to-event".
-Answer “Time-to-event” if the outcome can occur only once in a person in a clinical trial and is defined by the incidence per unit of time (e.g., “incidence of HIV infection per PY”)
-Exception: Answer “End value” for time to reach a threshold at the group level (e.g., time to recruit 60 participants)

#### Examples of statistical terms or tests used for time-to-event analysis, which indicate the Metric is “Time-to-Event”:
log rank or logrank
Cumulative event rate
Cumulative incidence rate (CIR)
Incidence rate or Incidence rate ratio (IRR)
Competing event
Competing risk analysis
Gray’s test
Cox regression or Cox proportional hazards
Hazard ratio
Kaplan-Meier
Accelerated failure time model
Schoenfeld residual test
Restricted mean survival time

#### Examples:
-Overall survival
-Time to progression
-Incidence rate of type 2 diabetes


### H. "Sum per person": The total amount of a continuous measure (e.g., drug or biomarker) over time.
-Might be the area under the curve which is often in pharmacokinetic studies.

#### Examples:
-Area Under the Curve From Time Zero to Extrapolated Infinite Time (AUCinf) of Factor VIII Activity
-Cumulative dose of drug administered
-Total drug cost per participant

### I. "Maximum per person": The maximum value of a non-binary score over time.
-Might be the maximum value in a concentration-time curve (i.e. C-max). 
-Requires explicit evidence that the maximum value is of interest (or largest, most, etc.).

#### Examples:
-Maximum Concentration of Factor VIII Activity

### J. "Minimum per person": The minimum value of a non-binary score over time.
-Might be the minimum value in a concentration-time curve (i.e., C-min).
-Requires explicit evidence that the minimum value is of interest (or smallest, least, etc.).

#### Examples:
-Minimum observed plasma concentration of ibrutinib

### K. Multiple metrics
-If multiple metrics form a single outcome derived through multiple calculations, answer the "Metric” that represents the last step of the calculation.

#### Examples
-Answer “Change from baseline” for “Change from baseline in gait stride variability”.
-Answer “Variability or average per person” for “Average daily number of cigarettes smoked”

### L. “Not valid”: Multiple outcomes are defined using different metrics.
-Not used for “Composite” outcomes

#### Examples of “Not valid” outcomes
-“Progression and time to progression”
-”Hospital admission and length of stay”

### M. ”Not reported”
An outcome is classified as "Not reported" if none of the previous definitions apply and there is insufficient information to clearly define the metric or analysis method.
1. Events reported or analyzed multiple times per participant (e.g., repeated events in a randomized clinical trial):
The answer is "Not reported" if there is insufficient information to determine whether the outcome is based on:
-The number of events per participant, or
-The number, proportion, or percentage of participants experiencing the event.
This includes outcomes described by terms like "Incidence", "Frequency", or "Rate".
2. Events reported or analyzed once per participant (e.g., single-event outcomes in a randomized clinical trial):
The answer is "Not reported" if there is insufficient information to determine whether the outcome will be analyzed using:
-The proportion of participants experiencing the event, or
-A time-to-event analysis (e.g., log-rank test).
This includes outcomes described by terms like "Incidence", "Frequency", or "Rate".
3. Repeatedly monitored events or activities (e.g., ongoing measures):
The answer is "Not reported" if it is unclear whether the outcome will be defined by:
-The total amount of the event or activity (e.g., total exposure), or
-The amount of time associated with the event or activity (e.g., time exposed).



If both "End value" and another metric are implied, answer the other metric rather than "End value".

If both “Rate of change (repeated measures)” and "Change from baseline" are implied, answer “Rate of change (repeated measures)”.

#3. The fields "Unit of Measure," "Outcome Analysis Parameter Type," "Statistical Analysis Method," and "Classes" provide details on how the outcome is analyzed and reported. These elements can help in defining the "Metric."

## “Unit of measure”: The attribute quantified by the numerical result
-”Percentage of participants” or “Proportion of participants”: Metric could be “Change from baseline”, ”End value”, or “Time-to-event”.
-”Score on a scale”, “Scores on a scale”, or “Units on a scale”: Metric could  be “Change from baseline”, “End value”, “Rate of change (repeated measures)”, or “Variability or average per person”.
-Phrases like "percentage change", "percent change", "change of", and "change in" indicate "Metric" might be "Change from baseline".
-If a type of event (e.g., "count of suicide attempts", "events per participant", "episodes per participant"), "Metric" is usually "Events per person".
-If "Unit of Measure" refers to a unit of time (e.g., "per day", "/year"), "Metric" is usually "Duration", "Time-to-event" or "End value".

## “Outcome Analysis Parameter Type”: Procedure used to estimate the between-group effect in a clinical trial
-Mentions statistical terms or tests that indicate the Metric is time-to-event: Metric is “Time-to-event”
-Slope: "Metric" is "Rate of change (repeated measures)".
-Contains the phrase "final value": "Metric" might be "End value".
-Contains the phrase "net": "Metric" might be "Change from baseline" or “Rate of change (repeated measures)”.
 -Relative rate, Rate ratio, or Rate difference, "Metric" might be "Events per person".

## “Statistical Analysis Method”: The statistical method used to compare results between-groups
-Mentions statistical terms or tests that indicate the Metric is time-to-event: Metric is “Time-to-event”
-“Poisson regression” or “Negative binomial”:, "Metric" might be "Events per person".
-If "Statistical Analysis Method" is ANCOVA, "Metric" might be “Change from baseline”.

## “Classes”
-If "Classes" lists different time points,  "Metric" might be "End value".
-If "Classes" includes change at a time point,  "Metric" might be "Change from baseline" or “Rate of change (repeated measures)”.
