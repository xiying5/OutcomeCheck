# Guidelines for identifying and defining “Cutoffs”
-In clinical trials, outcomes are defined for individual participants using multiple elements. 
-"Cutoffs" is one of those elements.
-The outcomes you will assess were all predefined in clinical trials. 

## 1. Definition of "Cutoffs": The specific numeric cutoff(s) or categories that define an outcome, or different levels of an outcome, at the individual participant level.

### How to Locate Cutoffs
1. Pay attention to any numerical values, thresholds, or categories mentioned.
2. Identify words and phrases such as "defined as," "categorized by," "threshold of," "greater than," "less than," "increase," "decrease," "reduction," "improvement," "score of," “at least,” “or higher,” etc.
3. Spot any numbers accompanied by operational signs (e.g., =, >, <, ≥, ≤) and units of measurement (e.g., mg/dL, mmHg).
4. Be attentive to mentions of standardized instruments or criteria (e.g., RECIST, NYHA classification) that might come with cutoffs.
5. Understand the clinical context to determine if certain phrases imply a cutoff, even if not explicitly stated.
6.Cutoffs might be listed in the “Classes”.

## 2. Steps
1. Identify all cutoffs mentioned.
2. Determine whether “Attribute categorized” and “Cutoffs” are applicable. 
-Consider whether each cutoff defines outcomes for individual participants.
-Ignore descriptions of how to interpret values on scales if those descriptions are not integral to the outcome definition.
-Ignore cutoffs or criteria used to define the group of participants who will be included in the analysis.
3. If applicable, name the “Attribute categorized” and the “Cutoffs”.
4. Determine whether cutoffs define one or multiple outcomes.

## 3. Answer "Not applicable" for "attribute categorized" and "cutoffs" if:
### 3.1. Participants are dichotomized into those with zero events (=0) or one or more events (“>=1”).

Examples:
-Number of people with adverse events
-Participants with injuries

### 3.2. The outcome is binary and the occurrence of the event is not defined by categorizing a continuous or ordinal scale or by further categorizing a categorical variable.

Examples of binary events:
-Death
-Study dropout
-Treatment discontinuation

Examples of events defined by categorizing a scale or categorical variable:
-“Clinical Global Impression of Change = Very much improved or much improved”
-”Tumor overall response defined as complete response (CR) or partial response (PR)”

### 3.3. The outcome is a binary event and the occurrence of the event might include categorizing a continuous or ordinal scale, or further categorizing a categorical variable, but no scale is mentioned.

Examples:
-”New onset diabetes”
-“Hypoglycemic event”
-”Hypertension diagnosis”

### 3.4. The only cutoffs reported are used to define the group of participants who will be included in the analysis.

Examples: 
-“Percent change from baseline in target-to-background ratio of an index vessel by FDG-PET/CT in subjects with baseline Lp(a)>=50mg/dL and Low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol>=100mg/dL.”
-”Initial TQ administration will determine eligibility (score greater than or equal to 40) for the TRTT.”
-”Time to all-cause mortality in patients with LVEF <50%.”

### 3.5. The cutoffs do not define the occurrence of an event and they do not indicate that participants will be categorized for analysis (e.g., cutoffs explain only how to interpret scores).

Examples:
-”Change in body temperature. Measure of heat associated with the metabolism of the body. Normal range is between 36.5-37.5 °C (97.7-99.5 °F).”
-”Pain Interference Score. PIS scores range from 0 to 10 where 0–3 represents ‘low interference’, 4–6 ‘Moderate interference,’ and 7–10 ‘High interference.’”

## 4. Name the “attribute categorized” and the “cutoffs”, if applicable. Applicable outcomes are those defined for each individual participant by:
-Categorizing a continuous or ordinal scale using cutoff(s),
-Further categorizing a categorical variable by collapsing some of the categories,
-Time to reach a specific threshold, or
-Time during a specific threshold or range.

## 5. Name the “attribute categorized” by the cutoff. 
Answer the questionnaire/instrument, physical property, biological variable, count of recurrent events, or length of time.

### 5.1 Avoid unnecessary or redundant words (e.g., don't add "status" after an event, don’t add “level” after a biomarker).

### 5.2 For naming standardized tools, questionnaires, tests, and instruments:
-Determine the full name based on all text provided.
-Answer both the full name and acronym (e.g., "Beck Depression Inventory (BDI)").
-Answer the subscale name if a subscale rather than a full scale is used.
-Include version numbers (e.g., "BDI-II").
-Include author name and year (e.g., "Beck 2014") if reported.
-For modified versions, indicate the modification (e.g., "Modified Ashworth Scale").
-Do not add "scale" or "subscale" unless it is part of the formal name.
-Do not add numbers (e.g., number of questions, score cutoffs) unless part of the formal name.
-Do not include details of the standardized tool (e.g., structure, content, scoring and interpretation, reliability, validity, administration) unless part of the formal name.

Examples: 
-For “Severe anxiety (HAM-A score=31–56)” answer “Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A)”
-For “Proportion of subjects with plasma HIV-1 ribonucleic acid (RNA) <50 copies/milliliter (c/mL) at Week 48” answer “Plasma HIV-1 ribonucleic acid (RNA)”
-For “Incidence of Grade 3 or Higher Adverse Events as Assessed by National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Version 4.0” answer “National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Version 4.0”
-For “Mild pain defined by Brief Pain Inventory pain severity score” answer  “Brief Pain Inventory - Pain Severity”

### 5.3 For outcomes incorporating different attributes with distinct cutoffs, include all attributes in your answer, separated by semicolons.

Examples:
-For “Composite endpoint of AP and total bilirubin: AP < 1.67 × ULN, ≥ 15% decrease in AP, and Total bilirubin ≤ ULN.” answer “Alkaline Phosphatase (AP); Total bilirubin”.

## 6. Rules for naming the “cutoffs”:
### 6.1 For binary categories: Name a single cutoff, range, or category defining the outcome (e.g., “<2”, “140-160”, “full response”).

Examples:
-For “Severe anxiety (HAM-A score=31–56)” answer “31-56”.
-For “endoscopic subscale (ESS) =0” answer “=0”.
-For “Incidence of Grade 3 or Higher Adverse Events as Assessed by National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Version 4.0” answer “ ≥ Grade 3”

### 6.2 For more than two cutoffs or categories of the same attribute: list all cutoffs or categories used in the analyses, separated by commas.

Example: For “Proportion of Participants Categorized by Glycemic Control Levels of < 6.0%, 6.0% to < 7.0%, and ≥ 7.0% HbA1c” answer “< 6.0%, 6.0% to < 7.0%, ≥ 7.0%”.

### 6.3 For categories that are collapsed into one, connect them with "or".

Example: For “Tumor response defined as complete response (CR) or partial response (PR)” answer “complete response (CR) or partial response (PR)”.

### 6.4 For binary events defined by scales, answer "Not reported" if an exact cutoff is not provided (e.g., “clinically meaningful difference” or “undetectable level” without a numerical definition).

Examples: 
-Undetectable viral load
-Proportion of people with decrease from baseline in weight
-High systolic pressure
-Clinical response defined by Clinical Pulmonary Infection Score
-Tumor response defined by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria

### 6.5 Include the unit of measurement when available (e.g., mmHg).

Examples: For “ANC less than 500/mm^3” answer “<500/mm^3”.

### 6.6 Always include the operational sign before a cutoff (e.g., =, >, <, ≥, ≤).

Example: For “Number of participants with Controlled Blood Pressure (<140/90 mm Hg)” answer “<140/90 mm Hg”.

### 6.7 Include direction of change (e.g., increase, decrease).

Examples: 
-For “Time to 50% Reduction in UPCR” answer “50% Reduction”.
-For “Proportion of subjects who gained from baseline ≥ 5 letters in the study eye” answer “≥ 5 letters gain”.

### 6.8 For outcomes incorporating different attributes with distinct cutoffs, include cutoffs in your answer, separated by semicolons.

Examples:
-For “Normal serum potassium between 3.5 and 5.0 mmol/L and an increase in serum bicarb of ≥3 mmol/L.” answer “3.5-5.0 mmol/L; ≥3 mmol/L increase”.
-For “Composite endpoint of AP and total bilirubin: AP < 1.67 × ULN, ≥ 15% decrease in AP, and Total bilirubin ≤ ULN.” answer “< 1.67 × ULN, ≥ 15% decrease; ≤ ULN”.


## 7. Count the “number of outcomes” defined by the cutoffs
### 7.1. “Not applicable”
-Outcomes with no cutoffs reported.
-Outcomes not defined by cutoffs.
-Outcomes with cutoffs that are “Not reported”.

### 7.2. One outcome
-Binary or dichotomous outcomes
-Time-to-event (survival) analyses
-Categorical outcomes for which any individual participant can fall into at most one of the multiple categories based on a single measure; in other words, the categories are independent.

Examples: 
-”Birth weight <2500g”
-”Time to resolution of fever (body temperature less than 37.0°C)”
-“Categorical heart rate with 3 levels: <60, 60-100, >100”


### 7.3. More than one outcome, count each outcome:
a. There are cutoffs for more than one binary or continuous outcomes that do not form a composite.

Example: “Objective response rate, complete response rate, and time to complete response. Response measured by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST).”

b. Cutoffs create categories for a single measure, and a single participant could fall into more than one of the categories (e.g., cutoffs have overlapping ranges).

Examples of cutoffs with overlapping ranges:
-“Proportions of participants with severe depression (BDI>29) and moderate or severe depression (BDI>20)”.
-”Proportion of subjects with plasma HIV-1 RNA <50 c/mL and <400 c/mL over time.”
-”Preterm birth <34 and <37 weeks gestation.”
-”Percentage of schedule fingerstick checks < 70 mg/dL, < 60 mg/dL, <50 mg/dL.”



c. >=2 groups defined by the cutoffs or categories are analyzed separately.

Example: “Time to glucose level >5.6 mg/dL and Time to glucose level from 4 to 5.5 mg/dL”
