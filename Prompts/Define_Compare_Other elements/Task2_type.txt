# 1. Guidelines for defining “Aggregation method”
-In clinical trials, outcomes are defined using multiple elements. 
-“Aggregation method” is how results for individual participants are aggregated and compared between groups. Possible values: "Continuous", "Categorical", "Continuous; Categorical", or "Not reported". 
-The outcomes you will assess were all predefined in clinical trials. 

# 2. Common aggregation method: Answer the typical aggregation method for this outcome in clinical trials

Answer "Continuous" if: the outcome is typically analyzed by mean/median.

Answer "Categorical" if the outcome is typically analyzed by proportion/rate.

# 3. Actual aggregation method: Look for explicit information in the text about the aggregation method in this clinical trial

## 3a. "Continuous" indicators:
### 1) "Outcome Measure", "Outcome Description", or "Time Frame" states explicitly that group means or medians are calculated or compared
#### Examples
-Difference in mean cognitive composite score 
-Average change from baseline Pain Score
-Mean fasting plasma glucose in both treatment groups 
-Median overall survival

### 2) "Outcome Measure", "Outcome Description", or "Time Frame"  mentions statistical methods appropriate for continuous outcomes
#### Examples of statistical methods appropriate for continuous outcomes
-Generalized linear mixed model (GLMM)
-Linear mixed model (LMM)
-Linear regression
-t-test
-Mann-Whitney U Test 
-Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test
-Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test
-Kruskal-Wallis
-Rank-sum
-Mean difference
-Standardized mean difference
-Analysis of variance (ANOVA)
-Repeated Measures (ANOVA)
-Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE)
-Multivariate Analysis of Variance (MANOVA)
-Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA)


### 3) "Measure Type" is "Mean", "Median", "Least Squares Mean", "Geometric Mean", or "Geometric Least Squares Mean"
### 4) "Measure of Dispersion/Precision" is "Standard Deviation", "Standard Error", "Full Range", "Inter-Quartile Range", or "Geometric Coefficient of Variation"
### 5) "Unit of Measure" refers to a score, unit on a scale, or events
### 6) "Measure Type" is "Number" and "Unit of Measure" refers to events (e.g., falls)
### 7) "Outcome Analysis Parameter Type" contains "mean difference" or "difference in mean"
### 8) “Statistical Analysis Method" mentions statistical methods appropriate for continuous outcomes (e.g., t test, ANOVA, linear regression, Wilcoxon test, or Kruskal-Wallis test)


## 3b. "Categorical" indicators: 
### 1) "Outcome Measure", "Outcome Description", or "Time Frame" explicitly states that the outcome is a group proportion or rate, or number of participants
#### Examples
-Mortality rate
-Number of participants with a complete pathological response
-Incidence of HIV infection
-Cumulative incidence of new onset diabetes
-Percentage of Participants With Improvement in the Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy - Hepatobiliary (FACT-Hep) Total Score
-Number of people with adverse events during the study
-Proportions of participants improved, not improved, and worse
-Likelihood of developing adverse events
-Probability of participant dropout

### 2) "Outcome Measure", "Outcome Description", or "Time Frame" mentions statistical terms or methods that estimate or compare group proportion/rate
#### Examples of statistical terms or methods appropriate for estimating or comparing group proportions/rates
-chi-squared
-logistic regression
-cox regression
-cumulative event rate
-incidence rate
-cumulative incidence
-competing risk analysis
-relative risk
-risk ratio
-risk difference
-odds ratio
-hazard ratio
-Exact Binomial Test
-Fisher’s exact test
-Mantel-Haenszel Test
-McNemar's Test
-log rank
-Poisson Regression
-Negative Binomial Regression

### 3) "Measure Type" is "Count of Participants"
### 4) "Measure Type" is "Number" and "Unit of Measure" refers to participants
### 5) "Unit of Measure" refers to participants or percentage of participants
### 6) "Outcome Analysis Parameter Type" mentions statistical terms appropriate for comparing group proportions/rates (e.g., "Odds ratio", "Cox proportional", or "hazard ratio")
### 7) “Statistical Analysis Method" mentions statistical tests appropriate for comparing group proportions/rates

## 3c. Answer "Continuous; Categorical” if both "Continuous" and "Categorical" indicators are mentioned.

## 3d. Answer "Not reported” if there is no "Continuous" or "Categorical" indicator.


## 3e. Additional notes
### 1) Ignore statements about the average follow-up time (e.g., mean follow-up of 3 years).

### 2) Proportions, percentages, rates, number of events calculated at the individual participant level rather than aggregated at the group level are not "Categorical" indicators (e.g., “Percentage of sessions completed by a participant”, "number of ICU admissions")

### 3) If "Measure of Dispersion/Precision" is a confidence interval, "Actual aggregation method" can be "Continuous" or "Categorical"
