# Guidelines for identifying and defining Timepoint:
-In clinical trials, outcomes are defined using multiple elements. "Timepoint" is one of those elements.
-You will assess the “Timepoint” of outcomes measured in clinical trials.

## Definition
"Timepoint”: The specific time(s) when an outcome will be assessed, including:
-A specific time (e.g., 1 week, 30 minutes post-dose); or 
-Duration of assessment of the participant (e.g., from admission to discharge, up to 1 week); or 
-Frequency of measurement (e.g., every 6 months).

## Steps
-Identify and evaluate time-relevant information in two sources for a clinical trial, the “prospective version” and the “last version”.
-Compare the “Timepoint” for each outcome to determine whether it changed between the first and second source.

## Identify time-relevant information for each outcome in each source
-Within each source determine the “Timepoint” using time-relevant information from "Time Frame", "Outcome Measure", "Outcome Description", and “Classes”.
-Differences within sources: "Timepoint” can be different from what is mentioned explicitly in "Time Frame".
-Not reported: Answer “Not reported” if no time-relevant information is available in the "Time Frame", "Outcome Measure", "Outcome Description", or "Classes".
-Ignore time 0 (“Baseline”):
a. Treat phrases indicating the start of the trial (“baseline”, “randomization”, “screening”, “enrollment”, “treatment initiation”, “the beginning”, “T0”) as synonyms of “0”.
b. Clinical trials sometimes imply that outcomes will be assessed at baseline using words such as “change”, “reduction”, “decrease”, “improvement”, “maintain”, “increase”, “enlargement”, “loss”, “gain”, “worsening”, or “growth”. 
c. Intervals might imply that outcomes were measured at baseline. Assume phrases like "within" and "up to" indicate the interval begins at baseline unless the text states that monitoring begins after baseline.

### Examples of time-relevant information in “Time Frame”
-“Timepoint” is “post-intervention at months 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48” for “Outcome Measure: Oswestry Disability Index, Outcome Description:, Time Frame:baseline & post-intervention at months 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48”.

### Examples of time-relevant information in “Outcome Measure”
-“Timepoint” is “5 years” for “Outcome Measure: 5-year disease free survival, Outcome Description:, Time Frame:.”
-“Timepoint” is “12 weeks” for “Outcome Measure: Rate of depressive symptoms as per Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) at 12 weeks on patient and family caregiver reported outcomes, Outcome Description:, Time Frame: 12 weeks”.

### Examples of time-relevant information in “Outcome Description”
-“Timepoint” is “16 weeks” for “Outcome Measure: Carotid Artery Intima Media Thickness (CIMT), Outcome Description: 9-13-MHZ B-mode Carotid Ultrasound will be used to assess intima media thickness at baseline and following 16 weeks of participation in an intensive behavioral weight loss intervention., Time Frame: 16 weeks”.

### Examples of time-relevant information in “Classes”
-“Timepoint” is “week 4, week 12, week 20, week 28” for “Outcome Measure: Adjusted Change From Baseline in Body Weight Through Week 28, Outcome Description:., Time Frame: Baseline (Day 1) up to Week 28, Class: week 4, week 12, week 20, week 28”.


## Guidelines for Comparing Timepoints 
Compare the start time point (except for baseline), end time point, and any intermediate time points between the two versions to identify any additions, deletions, or modifications:
1. More complete: "Timepoint" is "Not reported" in the prospective version but is reported validly in the last version.

2. Less complete: "Timepoint" is valid in the prospective version but is "Not reported" in the last version.

3. Answer “Change in definition” when:
-Times do not match
-A time (excluding baseline) is added or deleted in the last version compared with the prospective version.
-Additional information in either version meaningfully changes the timepoints or adds new information about the timing of assessments.

4. No change: "Timepoint" is semantically the same in both versions, even if there are minor differences in wording or specificity that don't alter the meaning.
