# Guidelines for Defining “Specific measurement” for a Single Score or Event

# 1. Objectives
Review an outcome registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. Identify the attribute(s) assessed, and identify the names of any tools or questionnaires used to assess the attribute(s).

## Steps:
a. Determine the attribute type, which might be:
-Directly Observable,
-Assessed by Standardized Device or Test, or
-Subjective or Complex.

b. If the attribute is Subjective or Complex, determine whether it is assessed using a:
-Objective or observable indicator,
-Indicator assessed by standardized device or test, or
-Specialized tool, questionnaire, test, or instrument.

c. Name the “Specific measurement”, which might be:
-The name of an attribute that is directly observable or assessed by standardized device or test, or 
-The name of a specialized tool, questionnaire, or instrument, or 
-“Not reported”

#2. Determine the attribute type
-An attribute is a characteristic or property of patients or subjects to be quantified, assessed, or categorized.
-An attribute is not the data source or data collection method (e.g., it is not electronic medical record, self-report, or chart review).

Examples of biological attributes: blood pressure, heart rate, glucose level.
Examples of psychological attributes: anxiety, depression, cognitive function.
Examples of behavioral attributes: smoking habits, exercise frequency, diet patterns.
Examples of clinical outcomes: survival, disease recurrence.

##2.1 Directly Observable
-Measured by direct observation without specialized tools, questionnaires, tests, or instruments. 
-Many research articles describing the attribute would not describe how it was assessed.
-Occurence of the event, or the initiation or completion of the action, could be assessed objectively and directly.
-Data are commonly collected from hospital, medical, and registry records.

-Diagnosis, remission, progression, or recurrence of a disease is usually not a directly observable attribute.
- A single symptom (e.g., cough) might be a directly observable attribute, but symptom severity and scores on questionnaires are not directly observable.

Examples:
-Mortality
-Hospitalization
-Undergoing mammography for breast cancer screening
-Vomiting
-Falling

##2.2 Assessed by Standardized Device or Test
-Assessed using common devices or tests considered interchangeable for research purposes.
-A research article describing the outcome would not normally name the manufacturer, model, or version of the device or test.
-Never a patient-reported outcome assessed using a questionnaire.

Example attributes:
-Height
-Weight
-Systolic blood pressure
-Heart rate
-Hemoglobin
-Serum creatinine
-Blood glucose

Example Standardized Devices and Tests:
-X-ray
-Spirometer
-Glucometer
-Blood test
-Microbiology test

##2.3 Subjective or Complex
-Cannot be observed directly or quantified objectively, including outcomes measured by objective or observable indicators, measured by attributes assessed by standardized devices or tests, and measured using specialized tools, questionnaires, psychological/cognitive tests, or instruments.
-Research articles  use different methods to measure the attribute. An article describing the outcome would normally name the questionnaire, model, version of the device or test, or clinical criteria.
-All patient-reported outcomes assessed using questionnaires.
-Diagnosis, remission, progression, or recurrence of a disease is often complex and not directly observable.

Examples:
-Cognitive functioning (e.g., memory, attention, language)
-Mental health conditions (e.g., depression, anxiety, stress)
-Quality of life
-Symptom severity (e.g., pain, fatigue)
-Functional status (e.g., daily activities)
-Psychosocial factors (e.g., social support)
-Knowledge about health problems or treatments
-Patient satisfaction with a medical decision or health care service

###2.3.1 Objective or observable indicator
-The attribute cannot be observed directly or quantified objectively, but it is assessed using an objective or observable indicator. 
-An attribute that could be assessed using an objective or observable indicator might not necessarily be assessed that way.

Examples:
-"Feasibility" measured by successful recruitment, which can be assessed through direct observation.
-"Adherence" measured by pill count, which is an observable indicator.
-“Healthcare utilization” measured by ICU admission, which is an observable indicator.

###2.3.2 Indicator assessed by standardized device or test
The attribute cannot be observed directly or quantified objectively, but it is defined by an indicator that is assessed by standardized devices or rests.

Examples:
-The attribute ”preterm birth” is defined by “gestational age”, which is assessed using standardized methods considered interchangeable for research.
-The attribute “virologic resuppression” is defined by “viral load”, which is measured using a common blood test (e.g., PCR) considered interchangeable for research.
-The attribute ”body composition” can be measured by “fat mass” and “lean body mass”, which are measured using common devices considered interchangeable for research (e.g, Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry).

###2.3.3 Specialized tool, questionnaire, test, or instrument
-The attribute is measured using specialized tools, questionnaires, psychological/cognitive tests, or instruments. Synonyms for “measured” include “evaluated”, “rated”, “assessed”, “graded”, and “defined”.
-Names of tools are often capitalized, preceded by the definite article, and may include words like "Scale", "Subscale", "Score", "Criteria", "Questionnaire", "Index", "Survey", "Instrument", "Test", etc., or be represented by acronyms (e.g., "BPI").


#3. Instructions for naming the "Specific measurement"
##3.1 The "Specific measurement" is the name of an attribute or indicator that is “Directly Observable” or assessed by a “Standardized Device or Test”: 

### Instructions for naming an attribute or indicator
a.Include the specific cause, type, source, or order if reported.

Examples:
-”systolic blood pressure” rather than “blood pressure”
-”serum IgG” rather than “antibody”
-”Hospitalization for depression” rather than “Hospitalization”
-”Mortality due to heart attack” rather than “Mortality”
-”Full term birth” rather than “Birth”
-”Antipsychotic initiation” rather than “Antipsychotic use”
-”Second pregnancy” rather than “pregnancy”

b.Do not include the following information: 
-Calculations and statistical concepts (e.g., "frequency," "number of," "percentage of", "rate," "area under the curve", "geometric mean ratio", "average", “count”)
-Time (e.g., "time to," "duration", "length", "days", "12-months")
-Comparison (e.g., "percent change", "change in," "improvement," "reduction", "maximum," "minimum")
-Operators and cutoffs (e.g., "at least 2% improvement", ">=8", "undetectable", "higher than", "lower than", "below the threshold")
-Unnecessary or redundant words (e.g., don't add "status" after an event, "level" after a biomarker).
-Data source or data collection method (e.g., electronic medical record, self-report, or chart review)
-Description of the population in which the measurement is taken

Examples:
-“Stent placement” rather than "number of stents placed"
-“Hospital discharge” rather than “time to discharge”
-“Hospital stay” rather than “length of hospital stay”
-“Pills taken” rather than “change in the pill count”
-“Body weight” rather than "change in body weight"
-“Height” rather than “rate of linear growth”
-“Systolic blood pressure” rather than “Systolic blood pressure<140 mmHg”
-“Viral load” rather than “Peak viral load”

##3.2  The "Specific measurement" is the name of a questionnaire or tool.

### Instructions for naming a tool or questionnaire

a. For a validated tool or questionnaire used commonly in clinical and research settings.
Determine the full name based on all text provided and by applying your knowledge of clinical trials.
-Answer the full name
-Include the acronym, if there is one.
-Include the subscale name if a subscale rather than a full scale is used.
-Include version number if reported.
-Include author name and year if reported.
-For modified versions, indicate the modification

Examples:
-Beck Depression Inventory (BDI-2), Beck 1996
-Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9)


Do not include the following unless part of the formal name: 
-The word "scale" or "subscale".
-Numbers (e.g., number of questions, score cutoffs).
-Details of the standardized tool (e.g., structure, content, scoring and interpretation, reliability, validity, administration).

b.  If the tool or questionnaire is not a validated instrument commonly used in clinical and research settings
Answer "Study-specific" followed by:
 -Number of questions (e.g., "1-question", "5-questions")
 -Question type (e.g., "Yes/No", "3-point Likert scale", "rating scale", "open-ended")
 -Attributes measured by the questions, named following instructions in “#3.1”.

##3.3 The "Specific measurement" is “Not reported” if:
a. The attribute is Subjective and Complex, and no directly observable indicator is mentioned.
b. The attribute is Subjective and Complex, and no indicator that is assessed by standardized devices or tests is mentioned.
c. The attribute is Subjective and Complex, and no validated questionnaire or tool is mentioned.
d.The attribute is subjective and complex,  and a non-standardized survey is mentioned, but neither number of questions nor question type are stated.

Rationale: The outcome measures the feasibility of recruiting older veterans with PTSD into a 12-week exercise program. It is assessed using an objective or observable indicator (section 2.3.1): the actual recruitment of veterans.

Naming the Specific Measurement: According to section 3.1, we should name the observable indicator without statistical terms or calculations. Avoid phrases like "number of veterans recruited" (since "number of" is a statistical concept to exclude).
Include specific details (section 3.1a): "older veterans with PTSD."

Specific measurement: "Recruitment"
