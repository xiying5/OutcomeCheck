# Guidelines for Defining “Specific measurement” for a Single Composite

# 1. Objectives
-You previously determined that the outcome to be assessed is a composite outcome.
-Your task now is to identify whether the outcome is a “Composite event” or a ”Multi-criteria event” .
-You then extract each component, separated by semicolons, or answer "Not reported" if the text does not list the components. 

# 2. Guidelines for defining "Composite type"
## “Composite event”
A “Composite event” is a single outcome defined by more than one component events connected by the logic “Or” (i.e., the composite event occurs if any of the component events occur).

#### Examples
-Progression-free survival
-Death or cardiovascular event
-Disease worsening or change in medication
-Recurrence or study withdrawal

## ”Multi-criteria event” 
Defined by at least two criteria connected by the logic “And” (i.e., the event occurs only if all criteria are met)
-Criteria are usually defined by different attributes or different measurements.
-Participants defined by a criterion would not be simply a subset of the group defined by another criterion.





# 3. Guidelines for defining "Components"

## 3.1 Steps
Extract each component, separating them with semicolons (;).
If the text does not list the components, write “Not reported.”

## 3.2 A component is an attribute that is directly observable or assessed by a standardized device or test, or the name of a specialized scale or instrument that quantifies a complex or subjective attribute.

Examples of directly observable attributes:
-Mortality
-Hospitalization
-Undergoing mammography for breast cancer screening
-Vomiting
-Falling

Examples of attributes assessed by standardized device or test:
-Height
-Weight
-Systolic blood pressure
-Heart rate
-Hemoglobin
-Serum creatinine
-Blood glucose

Examples of specialized scales or instruments:
-Beck Depression Inventory (BDI-2)


## 3.3 Instructions for naming the "Component"
### 3.3.1 The "Component" is the name of an attribute or indicator that is “Directly Observable” or assessed by a “Standardized Device or Test”: 

a.Include the specific cause, type, source, or order if reported.

Examples:
-”systolic blood pressure” rather than “blood pressure”
-”serum IgG” rather than “antibody”
-”Hospitalization for depression” rather than “Hospitalization”
-”Mortality due to heart attack” rather than “Mortality”
-”Full term birth” rather than “Birth”
-”Antipsychotic initiation” rather than “Antipsychotic use”
-”Second pregnancy” rather than “pregnancy”

b.Do not include the following information: 
-Calculations and statistical concepts (e.g., "frequency," "number of," "percentage of", "rate," "area under the curve", "geometric mean ratio", "average")
-Time (e.g., "time to," "duration of", "length of", "days of", "12-months")
-Comparison (e.g., "percent change", "change in," "improvement," "reduction", "maximum," "minimum")
-Operators and cutoffs (e.g., "at least 2% improvement", ">=8", "undetectable", "higher than", "lower than", "below the threshold")
-Unnecessary or redundant words (e.g., don't add "status" after an event, "level" after a biomarker).
-Data source or data collection method (e.g., electronic medical record, self-report, or chart review)
-Description of the population in which the measurement is taken

Examples:
-“Stent placement” rather than "number of stents placed"
-“Hospital discharge” rather than “time to discharge”
-“Hospital stay” rather than “length of hospital stay”
-“Pills taken” rather than “change in the pill count”
-“Body weight” rather than "change in body weight"
-“Height” rather than “rate of linear growth”
-“Systolic blood pressure” rather than “Systolic blood pressure<140 mmHg”
-“Viral load” rather than “Peak viral load”

### 3.3.2  The "Component" is the name of a questionnaire or tool.

a. For a validated tool or questionnaire used commonly in clinical and research settings:
Determine the full name based on all text provided and by applying your knowledge of clinical trials.
-Answer the full name
-Include the acronym, if there is one.
-Include the subscale name if a subscale rather than a full scale is used.
-Include version number if reported.
-Include author name and year if reported.
-For modified versions, indicate the modification

Examples:
-Beck Depression Inventory (BDI-2), Beck 1996
-Modified Ashworth Scale

Do not include the following unless part of the formal name: 
-The word "scale" or "subscale".
-Numbers (e.g., number of questions, score cutoffs).
-Details of the standardized tool (e.g., structure, content, scoring and interpretation, reliability, validity, administration).

b.  If the tool or questionnaire is not a validated instrument commonly used in clinical and research settings
Answer "Study-specific" followed by:
 -Number of questions (e.g., "1-question", "5-questions")
 -Question type (e.g., "Yes/No", "3-point Likert scale", "rating scale", "open-ended")
 -Attributes measured by the questions, named following instructions in “###3.3.1”.

### 3.3.3 Answer "Not reported" if components are not explicitly listed.
-Do not infer the components based on the name of the composite outcome.
